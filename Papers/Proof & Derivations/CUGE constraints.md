In the CUGE framework the vacuum response is fixed to the symmetric linear form

\[
\varepsilon(\Phi) = \varepsilon_0\left(1 + \frac{\Phi}{2c^2}\right), \qquad \mu(\Phi) = \mu_0\left(1 + \frac{\Phi}{2c^2}\right)
\]

(with dimensionless \(n(\Phi) \approx 1 + \Phi/(2c^2)\)). This is not an arbitrary choice. It is tightly constrained by three independent requirements that must be satisfied simultaneously in flat Euclidean 3-space + time. Any deviation in the coefficient or functional form immediately breaks at least one of them.

### 1. Impedance invariance (theoretical constraint — forces symmetry and the factor 1/2)

Global constancy of vacuum impedance

\[
Z_0 = \sqrt{\frac{\mu(\Phi)}{\varepsilon(\Phi)}} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu_0}{\varepsilon_0}}
\]

is required to eliminate reflections at mass boundaries and to preserve local Maxwell structure. This forces the response to be symmetric: any change in \(\varepsilon\) must be matched by an identical fractional change in \(\mu\). The unique first-order solution (positive-magnitude convention for \(\Phi\)) is exactly the factor \(1/2\) in front of \(\Phi/c^2\).

If the coefficient differed between \(\varepsilon\) and \(\mu\), \(Z_0\) would vary with position and reflections would appear — contradicting the absence of observed vacuum-boundary reflections and the framework’s claim of globally invariant impedance.

### 2. Local \(c\)-invariance (theoretical + experimental constraint — fixes co-scaling)

Atomic clocks and rulers must co-scale with the medium so that any local observer measures

\[
c_{\rm local} = 299792458~\rm m\,s^{-1}
\]

exactly, independent of gravitational potential. This requires:

- Transition energies \(E \propto 1/\varepsilon^2\),
- Bohr radius (ruler) \(a_0 \propto \varepsilon\),
- Effective Planck constant for detection thresholds \(h_{\rm eff} \propto 1/\varepsilon\) (photoelectric/ASH regime).

The combination automatically cancels when forming \(\lambda_{\rm meas} \times f_{\rm meas}\), restoring exact local \(c\). The specific coefficient \(1/2\) is required for the cancellation to be perfect while simultaneously producing the observed gravitational redshift split into equal kinematic and refractive halves (the “half-effect” tested by Sturm).

A different coefficient would either spoil local \(c\)-invariance or destroy the exact factor-of-2 split between kinematic and refractive contributions to redshift — both ruled out by experiment.

### 3. Weak-field GR tests (experimental constraint — fixes the effective refractive index)

All classical weak-field tests must be recovered exactly:

- Light deflection \(\theta = 4GM/(c^2 b)\),
- Shapiro delay (Cassini bound \(\sim 10^{-5}\)),
- Perihelion precession \(6\pi GM/[c^2 a(1-e^2)]\),
- Gravitational redshift (full \(-GM/(rc^2)\)).

These fix the *effective* refractive index felt by null geodesics (light) to

\[
n_{\rm eff}(r) = 1 + \frac{2GM}{c^2 r}
\]

(to first order). The constitutive response \(\varepsilon(\Phi)\), \(\mu(\Phi)\) with coefficient \(1/2\) produces exactly this \(n_{\rm eff}\) when both the time-dilation (kinematic) and spatial-gradient (refractive) contributions are included via Fermat’s principle in flat space. The doubling relative to the naive Newtonian value arises because both \(\varepsilon\) and \(\mu\) respond symmetrically.

A coefficient other than \(1/2\) would change the predicted deflection or Shapiro delay away from the observed values while still preserving impedance invariance — immediately ruled out by Cassini, VLBI deflection measurements, and Mercury’s perihelion.

### Additional constraints from the framework itself

- **VSS energy density and galactic dynamics**: The same linear response generates \(u_{\rm vac} = |\nabla\Phi|^2/(8\pi G)\), which must supply the extra dynamical mass \(M_{\rm vac} \approx 5M_b\) needed for flat rotation curves. The coefficient \(1/2\) is required for the non-linear Poisson equation to close consistently with the observed \(M_{\rm vac}/M_b\) ratio without new parameters.
- **Half-effect (Sturm 2022)**: Pure acceleration changes no vacuum properties (\(\varepsilon, \mu\) fixed). Only the kinematic (transverse Doppler) half appears. Gravity changes both halves. This directly constrains that acceleration does *not* induce the same \(\delta\varepsilon, \delta\mu\) as a real gravitational potential — consistent only with the CUGE form.
- **No free parameters**: The coefficient is completely fixed by the three requirements above. Introducing a free coupling (as in Brans–Dicke) would violate either impedance invariance, local \(c\)-invariance, or the exact match to the four classical GR tests.

### Summary of allowed form

In flat Euclidean space the only response compatible with all constraints to first order is the symmetric linear form with coefficient exactly \(1/2\):

\[
\varepsilon_r = \mu_r = 1 + \frac{\Phi}{2c^2}.
\]

Higher-order terms (non-linear feedback from \(u_{\rm vac}\)) are allowed and produce the refractive event horizon at \(r_s = 2GM/c^2\), but they are negligible in the weak-field regime where the above constraints are tested.

Any proposed modification to \(\varepsilon(\Phi)\) must simultaneously satisfy impedance invariance, local \(c\)-invariance, and the four weak-field GR tests to the precision of current data (especially Cassini at \(10^{-5}\)). The CUGE choice is the unique minimal solution that does so without introducing new parameters or violating the half-effect distinction between acceleration and gravity.
