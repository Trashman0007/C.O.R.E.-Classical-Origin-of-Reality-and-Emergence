## Abstract

We show that the Lorentz transformation follows from the invariance of the global impedance-matched vacuum electromagnetic phase cone. A vacuum plane wave has phase

$$
\phi = \vec{k}\cdot\vec{r} - \omega t

and satisfies the vacuum dispersion relation

$$

$$

\qquad
E = cB.
$$

Surfaces of constant phase for vacuum electromagnetic signals form the null phase cone

$$
x^2+y^2+z^2-c^2t^2 = 0.
$$

If inertial coordinate transformations are linear, reciprocal, isotropic, and preserve the coincidence of impedance-matched phase fronts, then they preserve this phase cone. The most general such transformations are the Lorentz transformations. The Lorentz factor

$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}
$$

is therefore the unique coefficient required to preserve the global impedance-matched vacuum phase cone under boosts.

The invariant speed $c$ is not introduced as an abstract kinematic postulate. It is the phase speed of the global impedance-matched vacuum field. The global impedance $Z_0$ is the ontological keystone of the C.O.R.E. framework: it fixes the electric-magnetic amplitude relation, defines the vacuum phase cone, and determines how every inertial observer interprets the invariant speed.

Refractive deformations of the phase cone, responsive-vacuum strain, and gravitational applications are outside the present scope and are deferred to the CUGE component of the C.O.R.E. framework. The present paper isolates the pure vacuum foundation of special relativity within C.O.R.E.

$$
X'\cdot X' = 0.

$$
X'\cdot X' = \Omega(\Lambda)\,(X\cdot X)

$$
\Omega(v)=1.

# 6. Explicit Boost Form and the Factor $\gamma$

$$

\qquad

To see how $\gamma$ is fixed, write the general boosted form as

$$

$$

$$
t' = a x + b t.
$$

Demanding invariance of

$$
x^2-c^2t^2

gives

$$
x'^2-c^2t'^2 = x^2-c^2t^2.

Substitution yields the conditions

$$
\gamma^2 - c^2a^2 = 1,

$$
\gamma^2 v + c^2ab = 0,

$$
\gamma^2v^2 - c^2b^2 = -c^2.
$$

The reciprocal solution is

$$
a = -\gamma\frac{v}{c^2},
\qquad
b = \gamma.
$$

The first invariance condition then becomes

$$
\gamma^2\left(1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}\right)=1,
$$

so

$$
\gamma =
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}}.
$$

Thus $\gamma$ is not an independent dynamical postulate. It is the unique numerical coefficient that allows a linear, reciprocal boost to preserve the global impedance-matched vacuum phase cone.
